Designing beyond the screen.

A robotics concept connects perception, decisions and physical action.

Robotics / STUDIO NOTES

Describe the physical task

Start with what the machine needs to do in the real environment. Identify the objects involved, the available space, the expected conditions and the people nearby. “Move a known object between two fixed positions” is a clearer starting point than “build an intelligent assistant”. A precise task makes assumptions visible and helps a team choose an appropriate prototype.

Connect perception to action

A robotics experience needs a way to sense relevant conditions, decide what to do and carry out that decision. Consider each step separately. A confident software response is not evidence that a physical action is appropriate. The design must account for uncertainty, missing information and the difference between a simulated environment and a real one.

Plan human involvement

Decide when the machine should request help, stop or return control to a person. Make status understandable: what is it doing, what is it waiting for and what can the person do next? These are interaction-design questions as well as engineering questions. Define responsibilities before attempting a more autonomous workflow.

Prototype with clear limits

Use a constrained demonstration to investigate one question at a time. Record the environment and conditions in which the prototype was evaluated rather than treating a successful demonstration as proof of general readiness. Hardware selection, mechanical design and real-world validation require domain expertise. A useful concept makes both the possibility and the remaining engineering work clear.
