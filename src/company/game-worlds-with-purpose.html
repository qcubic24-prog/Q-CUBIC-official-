Build a world that tells the player what matters.

Environment design works best when atmosphere and interaction support each other.

Gaming / STUDIO NOTES

Start with the player’s question

When a player enters a space, they need to understand what is possible. Where can they go? What can they interact with? What might be dangerous? An environment can answer these questions through scale, light, composition and repeated visual cues. A dramatic scene should still provide a readable path through the experience.

Use a consistent visual language

Decide which shapes and materials suggest safety, movement, barriers or interaction. Repeating these decisions lets players learn the world without reading instructions at every turn. Reserve strong contrasts for things that deserve attention. If every surface demands attention, the important action can become difficult to find.

Prototype movement early

Explore a space with simple geometry before committing to detailed artwork. Test sightlines, camera behaviour, traversal distances and the rhythm between open and enclosed areas. Animation and camera decisions can change how a room feels, so review them together. A compelling still image is not enough evidence that a space works in motion.

Let detail reinforce the experience

Add detail where it supports story or interaction. A worn handle can suggest use; a changed lighting cue can signal a new state. Review the environment on the intended screen size and performance target. The aim is not maximum detail everywhere, but a world whose visual choices help the player understand and enjoy what they are doing.
